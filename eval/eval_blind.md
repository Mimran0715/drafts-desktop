# Blind Evaluation

## Rubric

For each prompt, score **Response A** and **Response B** 1-5 on:

1. **Physical/spatial coherence** - could this actually happen in space, with limbs/objects where the text says they are?
2. **Absence of purple prose / cliches** - concrete vs. vague-evocative language
3. **Sensory concreteness** - specific, grounded detail vs. generic description
4. **Constraint adherence** - did it actually follow the prompt's stated constraints?

Then note an overall preference (A / B / tie) per prompt. Don't open eval_key.json until you're done scoring.

## p1_physical_confrontation

**Prompt:** Write a scene where two characters argue in a cramped elevator that has stopped between floors. Track the physical position of both characters and how the confined space constrains their movement. Avoid metaphors; use precise spatial description.

### Response A

As the elevator lurched to a stop, Emma's eyes snapped towards hers, her expression a mixture of frustration and annoyance. Alex stood opposite her, his shoulders pinching against the metal walls as he tried to pace back and forth.

Emma shifted her weight onto one hip, her foot tapping impatiently on the cold floor. The confined space made her feel claustrophobic; she longed for fresh air and a sense of freedom. She glanced up at Alex, who was now forced to stand with his back against the wall, his eyes fixed intently on hers.

"You're really going to do this, aren't you?" Emma's voice rose in incredulity as she took a step closer to him, her shoulder blades pressed against the metal rail. Her hands fisted at her sides, as if she wanted to strike something – or someone.

Alex's eyes never wavered from hers, but his jaw clenched and unclenched, betraying his growing irritation. He inched forward, his chest almost touching the wall, but he couldn't move any further without bumping into Emma. His hands remained at his sides, fists loosening and tightening with each passing moment.

"I'm not going to apologize for what I did," Alex said, his voice low and even, but laced with a hint of defensiveness.

Emma's eyes flashed with anger as she took another step closer, her face mere inches from Alex's. Her knees touched the wall now, and she had to force herself to keep standing upright. "You never apologize, do you? You just expect everyone else to swallow your crap and move on."

As they stood frozen in a silent standoff, the air grew thick with tension. The confined space seemed to amplify their emotions, making every movement feel forced and unnatural. Emma's foot tapped faster, while Alex's eyes darted from hers to the stuck elevator doors.

The silence stretched, a fragile thread connecting them. It was as if they were waiting for someone – or something – to break the impasse. But in this cramped, motionless world, it seemed that only one of them would be willing to take the first step towards resolving their differences.

## p2_subtext_dialogue

**Prompt:** Write a dinner-table conversation between a parent and adult child where neither says what they actually mean. Focus on subtext - what each line is really doing beneath the surface meaning. Avoid explicit narration of emotions; let word choice and evasion carry the subtext.

### Response A

The dining room was still, the only sound the soft clink of silver against porcelain. Father held his fork over a small portion of roasted chicken, not taking it yet. Mother had already finished her main course and now sipped herbal tea, her hands folded neatly in her lap.

Father: “It’s funny how much food prices have increased since you moved out.”

*Subtext:* A casual opening line about money, but the mention of “moved out” is a loaded term. It implies independence, financial stability (or lack thereof), and a shift in responsibility—both for him to provide support and for her to demonstrate self-sufficiency.

Mother: “Yes. I was thinking of looking into a CSA share again this spring. That’s one way to avoid the supermarket markup.”

*Subtext:* She pivots to sustainability, a values-based choice that also happens to be cost-effective. The mention of the community-supported agriculture program (“CSA”) is code for her wanting to create her own food network and reduce reliance on commercial channels—both financially and emotionally.

Father: “A share? That sounds like a lot of commitment. You should consider just buying what you need at Safeway.”

*Subtext:* The tone is one of practical, thrifty concern, but the suggestion is an implicit rebuke. He’s framing her desire for connection to the land and a community as overly romanticized or impractical. It’s also a veiled commentary on her perceived lack of stability in her adult life—a need to be “practically” provisioned.

Mother: “But then you’d miss the seasonal varieties! The carrots this time of year are just—”

She cuts herself off, the sentence trailing into silence.

*Subtext:* She’s about to get into a detailed discussion of agricultural cycles and the sensory pleasures of eating seasonally. It would be a beautiful, authentic topic for them to bond over. But she stops, realizing the conversation has already become tense around financial responsibility and stability. By leaving the thought dangling, she subtly shifts the focus back onto aesthetics (“just”) rather than substance.

Father: “You should really get an apartment closer to the office. That commute is ridiculous.”

*Subtext:* The word choice is significant—“closer” implies a geographic, logistical necessity, not a personal preference. But the implication is clear: if she’s smart about her housing, she won’t be wasting so much time and money on travel. It’s an unspoken critique of her current living arrangement as inefficient.

Mother: “It’s fine for now. And it gives me extra time to… focus on my own projects.”

*Subtext:* She offers no defense of the commute itself, just a vague statement about having time for “her own projects.” This is not just an excuse; it’s a declaration of agency and purpose outside of her traditional role (employee, consumer). The ambiguity in what those projects are is deliberate—she leaves room for him to project his own ideas onto them (“You’re finally pursuing that art thing”) or dismiss the entire concept as idle.

Father: “I just worry you’re not setting yourself up right. You know I always wanted a house with a yard.”

*Subtext:* The sentence is phrased in terms of abstract security, but the image conjured is one of traditional domestic stability. A house = permanence. A yard = family. It’s an emotional appeal, using his own childhood nostalgia to suggest that she’s missing out on this fundamental part of adult life—having a stable home base.

Mother: “I’m happy where I am.” She says it softly, almost as if apologizing for her contentment.

*Subtext:* Her statement is not a declaration but a placating offering. The word “happy” is a diminutive, a way to signal that her choices aren’t “big” or ambitious, just… okay. The implication is: I’m settling for this small thing; don’t worry too much about me. It’s the opposite of assertiveness.

Father: “You know I’d help you with the mortgage if it was too tight.”

*Subtext:* He thinks he’s offering support, but in reality, he’s placing a massive conditional responsibility on her shoulders. If she accepts this offer, she’ll be forever tied to him as an adult dependent; if she rejects it, she’ll be seen as ungrateful and unable to handle the financial burden of adulthood. Either way, she loses agency.

Mother: “It’s all fine. Let’s talk about you. What’s new with work?” She changes the subject abruptly, turning her attention to him with a forced brightness.

*Subtext:* This is not kindness; it’s strategic retreat. By shifting the focus from *her* life choices (housing, CSA, projects) back to *his*, she’s effectively avoiding further conflict about her independence and self-sufficiency. She lets his anxiety be the primary concern for now, allowing herself a temporary reprieve from the pressure of being an “adult.” The tone is artificial cheer, a performance designed to diffuse tension.

Father: “It’s been good. We got that new contract.” He pauses, looks at her with what he thinks is gentle concern. “You should really start thinking about… retirement planning.”

*Subtext:* He’s not talking about his own retirement (he’s probably still working full-time). He’s using the abstract concept of long-term financial security to comment on *her* perceived lack of forward thinking and provision for her future—i.e., old age, which he associates with dependence. It’s a veiled commentary on her failure to plan for her “own” future, rather than his own.

Mother: “Retirement planning? You mean like saving up for… for when we’re older?” Her smile is frozen, her eyes scanning the room.

*Subtext:* She knows what he means, but she doesn’t play along. The phrase “for when we’re older” is a deflection—she’s not committing to an individualized plan; she’s framing it as a joint future (we). This subtle shift in pronoun is her quiet way of reasserting their relationship dynamic: she is still the child/daughter, he is still the parent/father. Her refusal to engage with the idea of “her own” retirement planning reinforces this boundary.

The silence that follows is thick and unspoken. Father’s fork taps against his plate once, twice, three times. Mother stares into her teacup, the liquid calm. The subtext has consumed the conversation entirely; the surface-level discussion about food, housing, and financial planning is a mere prop for all the unsaid things that now hover in the air between them—questions of identity, purpose, security, and who is still providing for whom.

No one takes another bite.

## p3_chase_uneven_terrain

**Prompt:** Write a foot chase across a rooftop under construction, with exposed beams and unfinished flooring. Track exact footing, balance, and near-falls for both the pursuer and the pursued. Avoid cinematic flourish; focus on kinesthetic detail.

### Response A

**Scene:** Rooftop under Construction, Late Afternoon.
**Setting:** A partially finished flat-roofed commercial building, circa 1970s architecture. The pursuit takes place along a long, narrow central spine where two wings of the building meet. The surface is exposed: 2x8 wooden beams laid perpendicular to the slope, with gaps between them; a thick layer of concrete aggregate (masonry sand, small gravel) provides footing on top of the deck plating. No permanent flooring or railing exists.

**Pursued (Person A):**
**Initial Footing:** Standing on a 3-inch gap between two beams. Feet planted side-by-side for stability.

1. **Step 1:** Lifts left foot and places it deliberately on the next beam forward, a solid 2x8 plank. Weight shifts fully onto left leg.
2. **Step 2:** Shifts weight back to right leg, bends knees slightly to maintain balance over the gap.
3. **Step 3:** Pushes off with both legs, lands center on the same beam as before (A’s footprints: L-R-L-R). The soft concrete aggregate beneath absorbs the impact.

**Pursuer (Person B):**
**Initial Footing:** Crouched at the edge of a gap, body low over the concrete. Right hand braced on an adjacent beam for support.

1. **Step 1:** Leans forward from crouch to spring onto the beam directly ahead. Both hands reach out simultaneously and grasp the next beam in the line.
2. **Transfer of Weight:** Shifts weight from both arms to right foot, then left, landing in a crouched stance on the new beam (B’s footprints: R-L).
3. **Step 2:** Takes three rapid steps along the beam, hands still gripping adjacent beams for balance and counter-balance. Each step is shorter, more controlled.
4. **Closing the Gap:** B takes one longer stride, landing with a soft thud on the same beam as A. The distance between their feet closes to approximately 18 inches.

**Action:**
A realizes B’s closing. A shifts weight fully onto left leg, bends knees to prepare for a jump over any remaining gaps or uneven terrain.

B sees the slight dip in A’s posture and misjudges the distance. B attempts one final lunge, stepping with their right foot into an unseen gap between two planks of different lengths. The beam underfoot gives slightly.

**Near-Fall (Pursued):**
A’s left knee flexes to absorb the sudden drop. Weight is transferred fully to the bent leg, which buckles at the ankle but holds. A reaches with the right hand, grabs a narrow 1x4 wooden trim piece running along the edge of the deck, fingers digging into the splintered surface.

**Near-Fall (Pursuer):**
B’s right foot slams down on the concrete aggregate between beams. The soft material offers little resistance; B’s entire body weight now rests on the left hand gripping the beam and the right leg planted solidly but with no secure footing below. B’s hips are cocked at an unnatural angle, straining against the imbalance.

**Recovery:**
A pushes off the trim piece, using it as a pivot point to swing their right foot forward, planting it on the next available beam 18 inches away. The momentum is halted; A is now standing upright but slightly crouched for stability.
B, realizing the danger, releases the left-hand grip and plants both palms flat against the cold, oily concrete deck, sliding backward in a controlled slide of approximately 2 feet. When they stop, their torso is twisted, right shoulder leading the way, and they regain a secure three-point stance on the next available beam.

**Aftermath:**
The two are now separated by 8-10 feet of uneven terrain, with B slightly higher up due to the slide. Both remain low, crouched for balance over the gaps and irregularities.

B’s breathing is heavy and audible from this distance. Their movements are now slower, more deliberate. Each step is planned ahead: right foot forward, weight transferring to that leg before committing fully.

A, seeing the pause in B’s advance, begins to circle around a large pile of concrete pavers (future planters), moving diagonally across their own path. They use the mass of the pavers as partial cover, peering out from behind each row or individual block.

B follows this new trajectory, but now must navigate through the stacked pavers. Each step becomes an exercise in finding a stable surface: sometimes the edge of a paver, sometimes a 1-inch gap between two pavers’ tops. They must use both hands to push off and balance.

**Kinesthetic Notes for Pursued (A):**
* Every footfall is a conscious act of weight transfer onto one leg while the other leg extends as a propeller.
* The soft aggregate beneath provides some shock absorption, but also requires constant micro-adjustments in foot placement to avoid slipping on the fine gravel.
* The 1x4 trim piece is used as an impromptu handhold; fingers dig into the wood’s coarse grain texture.
* A’s balance is constantly a slight lean forward from the hips, compensating for the lack of railing or guard. Any loss of focus causes the entire torso to shift back and up in a defensive hunch.

**Kinesthetic Notes for Pursuer (B):**
* B’s pursuit becomes a series of rapid, controlled lunges and then sudden halts as they assess the terrain.
* When crouching to step across gaps, B’s center of gravity is constantly shifting between arms, legs, and torso, requiring continuous micro-adjustments of grip and weight distribution.
* The concrete deck provides zero support for footfalls; every impact must be absorbed by a bent knee or a braced hand. A slip would result in a rapid slide backward with no control over direction.
* B’s body is now a study in tense economy: legs extended for propulsion, arms outstretched to catch balance, torso held in a constant 30-degree forward lean to counter the lack of overhead support. Any fatigue will cause their entire posture to sag and their footing to become more unpredictable.

**Prompt:** Write a tense standoff between a shop owner and a debt collector across a counter. Track hand positions, what each is holding or reaching for, and the physical objects between them. Keep dialogue clipped and avoid melodrama.

### Response A

The shop owner, Mrs. Patel, stood behind her worn wooden counter, her hands grasping the edge of it tightly as she faced off against debt collector, Mr. Jenkins. The air was thick with tension as they stared each other down.

Mrs. Patel's eyes narrowed, her gaze fixed on the stack of unpaid bills and invoices spread out before her. "You're not here to collect, Mr. Jenkins," she said firmly. "You're here to intimidate."

Mr. Jenkins smirked, his hands resting casually in his pockets as he leaned against the counter, his eyes never leaving Mrs. Patel's face. "Just doing my job, ma'am." He flashed a fake smile.

Mrs. Patel's grip on the counter tightened. She reached for a nearby ledger, her hand hovering above it before she pulled back. Her eyes flicked to the clock on the wall. 5:45 PM - closing time was just an hour away.

Mr. Jenkins straightened up, his hands still in his pockets as he took a step closer to the counter. "You know you're in breach of contract, Mrs. Patel. The bank is breathing down my neck."

Mrs. Patel's eyes flashed, her voice rising slightly. "I've got nothing to pay with. Not since the factory shut down." Her hand hovered over the phone as if considering calling for backup.

Mr. Jenkins chuckled low in his throat. "You're not going anywhere, ma'am. Not until you settle this debt." He patted his pocket where a small notebook and pen lay waiting.

The standoff continued, each side unwilling to back down. The air was thick with stale coffee and the faint scent of fabric softener wafting from the nearby sewing machines.

    *   A small, black notebook ledger tied shut with twine.

**CHARACTER DYNAMICS:**
*   **SHOP OWNER (M):**
    +   Position: Backed against a metal shelf filled with packaged goods, right hand holding a heavy ceramic mug of cold tea.
    +   Focus: Fixed on the ledger tied with twine. Right index finger tracing a line in the margin.
*   **DEBT COLLECTOR (C):**

1.  **Initial Stance**

    *   M does not look up from the ledger. Right thumb and forefinger loosen the tea mug’s ceramic handle, but don’t move it.

2.  **Counter Distance**
    *   C: “The interest is compounding now.”
    *   C slides their left hand an additional 15 cm across the counter, resting a palm on a tray of cookies.

3.  **Object Focus**
    *   C: “I don’t see your current accounts here. This is just debt notes.”

4.  **Reaching In**
    *   C: “I’ll take this as evidence of non-compliance.” The left hand extends further into the store.

5.  **Blocking Space**
    *   C: “You can’t stop me.”
    *   C steps forward, creating a diagonal path across the shop floor toward M.
    *   M does not back down. Instead, they push off from the shelf and take two short steps diagonally across their own counter space to create an identical triangle, blocking the direct path but leaving C with only the wider side aisles for approach.

6.  **Physical Confrontation (Avoided)**
    *   C’s body freezes; their gaze drops to where M’s left hand covers their own wrist. C’s right hand lowers, but does not put it in a pocket or purse.
    *   C: “I am here on legal notice.”

    *   C slowly withdraws the left arm from across the counter, returning it to the hip.

7.  **Resolving Posture**
    *   C: “The account is being sent to collections tomorrow.” The statement lacks dramatic flourish; it is a simple announcement.
    *   M gives one slow nod. Right hand releases its death grip on the mug handle; the mug now rests flat and still in their palm. Left hand retracts from C’s wrist, fingers straightening as it returns to the shelf.

8.  **Departure**
    *   C turns and walks out into the store without another word.
    *   M remains still, back against the metal shelf. Their right arm rests at their side now. They watch the door close behind C through the glass of the entrance.
    *   Only then does M’s left hand return to rest on the notebook ledger. The tea mug remains untouched on their right palm.

**END SCENE SKELETON**

The tension is a physical pressure, a silent contest played out across the width of the counter and the empty space between them. Every gesture—the reach, the point, the cover—was an attempt to control the space, but neither could take it without acknowledging the other’s presence. The objects on the counter were neutral, yet their placement (tray of snacks, jar of coffee beans) subtly framed each party: the snack tray offered a casual, everyday transaction; the coffee beans represented work, labor, and the slow grind of time; the ledger was the ultimate, unyielding authority. In the end, both sides retreated back into their respective roles—the debt collector leaving to file papers, the shop owner returning to the quiet routine of restocking shelves—but the memory of the standoff would be etched in the dust motes and the faint scent of freshly brewed coffee between them.
